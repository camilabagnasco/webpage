Fermentation

How to Make Your First
Wild Fermented Sauerkraut

April 3, 2025 12 min read Beginner-friendly

Fermentation is one of humanity's oldest food traditions — and one of the easiest ways to transform simple vegetables into gut-nourishing, living foods. This guide walks you through everything you need to begin.

[ Cover Image: 1200×675px, warm natural photography ]

Why Fermented Foods Belong in Every Kitchen

Wild fermentation relies on the beneficial bacteria already present on vegetables and in the air around us. Unlike yogurt or commercial probiotics, wild fermented foods create a diverse, living ecosystem of microorganisms that support the gut microbiome in ways science is only beginning to fully understand.

The beautiful thing about sauerkraut is that it requires just two ingredients — cabbage and salt — and yet it contains more beneficial bacteria than most expensive probiotic supplements on the market.

✦ Good to Know

Use non-iodized salt (sea salt or kosher salt) for fermentation. Iodized salt inhibits the growth of beneficial bacteria and can result in failed fermentation.

What You'll Need

The beauty of home fermentation is its simplicity. You need a clean glass jar (wide-mouth mason jars work perfectly), fresh cabbage, salt, and patience. That's it. No special equipment, no starter cultures, no guesswork.

Step 1 — Shred and Massage

Thinly slice the cabbage and place in a large bowl. Add 2% of its weight in salt (about 1 tsp per 300g). Massage firmly for 8–10 minutes until significant liquid is released.

✦ · · ✦ · · ✦

Your Fermentation Timeline

Most sauerkraut reaches optimal flavor and beneficial bacteria count between 7 and 21 days at room temperature. Taste it daily after day 5 to find your preferred level of tanginess.

Where to build it

An honest comparison of the best platforms for this type of blog, from free to professional.

WordPress.org
Self-hosted via Hostinger or SiteGround
~$3/month
  • Full monetization (ads, affiliates, digital products)
  • Own domain (yourblog.com)
  • Full plugin access (SEO, newsletter, ecommerce)
  • Industry standard for long-term blogs
  • Themes: Kadence, Astra, GeneratePress (free)
  • Small monthly cost ($2–5/mo)
  • Slight learning curve
Best Choice for Growth
Framer
Design-first website builder
Free (paid from $14/mo)
  • Most beautiful design output
  • Free tier available
  • Fast and modern

  • Less SEO control than WordPress

Free (with limits)
  • Truly free to start

  • Not suitable for serious long-term growth
